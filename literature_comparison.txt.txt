| **Artificial intelligence in higher education: the state of the field** <br><br> *Helen Crompton & Diane Burke* | To provide an up-to-date systematic overview of how AI was used in higher education (HE) between 2016 and 2022. | • Systematic review using **PRISMA principles** and **PRISMA-P protocol**.<br>• Searched 5 databases (EBSCOhost, Wiley, JSTOR, ScienceDirect, WoS) and 3 AIEd journals.<br>• Analyzed **138 peer-reviewed articles**.<br>• Used *a priori* (deductive) coding for metadata and grounded (inductive) coding for AI uses. | • AIEd publications grew **two- to three-fold in 2021–2022**.<br>• **China surpassed the USA** as the leading publisher.<br>• **Education departments** became the largest author affiliation group (28%).<br>• **72% focused on undergraduate students**; language learning was the most studied subject.<br>• **5 main AI uses**: Assessment/Evaluation, Predicting, AI Assistants, Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS), and Managing Student Learning. | • Included only peer-reviewed journal articles (excluded conference papers, books, and gray literature).<br>• Limited to English-language publications.<br>• Excluded empirical ChatGPT studies because published papers had not cleared review pipelines by late 2022. | • Uses the **PRISMA systematic review framework**.<br>• Identifies predictive modeling and virtual assistants as key functional roles of AI.<br>• Notes the growing leadership of Education departments in guiding AI research. | • **Strict Higher Education Focus**: Analyzes AI applications exclusively within university settings (72% undergraduates).<br>• **7-Year Chronological Analysis**: Tracks multi-year publication trends (2016–2022) before and during COVID-19 lockdowns.<br>• **Grounded Functional Framework**: Establishes 5 broad functional roles for AI tools in university management and teaching. |
